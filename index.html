Practice areas

Where we help

We serve outside and in-house counsel as consulting and testifying experts, from early case assessment and agency review through expert reports, depositions, and trial.

Antitrust

Economic analysis of mergers and business conduct for agency investigations and private litigation.

Mergers and acquisitions
Market definition, market shares and concentration, unilateral and coordinated effects, merger simulation, efficiencies and pass-through, and responses to agency data requests and economic theories of harm.
Vertical issues
Input foreclosure, incentives to raise rivals’ costs, and the elimination of double marginalization.
Conduct and monopolization
Tying, exclusive dealing, refusals to deal, and buyer-side (monopsony) effects, including effects on suppliers and workers.
Cartels and coordination
The effect of alleged price-fixing, output restriction, or market allocation on prices, output, and discounting.
Market definition and competitive effects
Hypothetical monopolist tests, diversion and closeness of competition, and empirical work with transaction, claims, survey, and geographic data.

Class action

Economic analysis at class certification and on the merits, for plaintiffs or defendants.

Class certification
Whether common economic evidence and methods can establish impact and damages across the proposed class under Rule 23.
Common impact
Regression and pricing-structure analysis testing whether the alleged conduct affected all or nearly all class members.
Damages
But-for prices and overcharges, structural-break and before-and-after analysis, and aggregate and individual damages models.
Rebuttal
Review and critique of opposing experts’ data, methods, and models.

Our team

The economists

Robert (Bob) Arons, Ph.D.

Managing Economist

Former Staff Economist, U.S. Department of Justice Antitrust Division

Ph.D. in Economics, University of Rochester

Bob Arons spent six years as a staff economist in the Antitrust Division of the U.S. Department of Justice (2014–2020). There he analyzed mergers and conduct, including market definition and competitive effects, led teams of economists, and received the Assistant Attorney General’s Award in 2015, 2017, and 2019. He then joined Secretariat Economists (formerly Economists Inc.) as a Senior Economist and later Associate Director, where he analyzed alleged cartels’ effects on price and output, performed regression analysis for class certification, and signed an expert report submitted to the Competition Commission of India. His work spans merger reviews in health insurance, telecommunications and media, and technology, as well as cartel and class action matters in agriculture, building materials, and healthcare. He holds a Ph.D. in economics from the University of Rochester, has taught industrial organization at Georgetown University, and serves as economics editor of the ABA’s Media and Technology News e-Bulletin.

Ross Brater, Ph.D.

Senior Economist

Former Economist, Economic Analysis Group, U.S. Department of Justice Antitrust Division

Ph.D. in Economics, The Ohio State University

Ross Brater is an antitrust economist who spent about a decade in the Economic Analysis Group of the U.S. Department of Justice Antitrust Division, from 2014 to 2024. His Division work centered on merger analysis, and he served on the economic teams for the Division’s Aetna–Humana and T-Mobile/Sprint merger matters, both of which were recognized with Assistant Attorney General’s Awards. He also prepared an expert report on bid-rigging overcharges that the government submitted at sentencing in United States v. Nichols (S.D. Ind. 2019), a public-corruption case covered by the Indianapolis Star. He earned his Ph.D. in economics from The Ohio State University, where his dissertation examined how loyalty rewards programs create switching costs and affect competition and entry. He also holds an undergraduate degree in mathematics and economics from Ohio Wesleyan University and has taught principles of economics at NYU Washington, DC.

John Baynes, Ph.D., CFA

Senior Economist

CFA Charterholder; former Assistant Professor of Economics and Finance, Canisius University

Ph.D. in Economics, University of Rochester

John Baynes is an economist whose career spans investment banking, academia, and corporate finance. He holds a Ph.D. and an M.A. in economics from the University of Rochester, where his dissertation developed methods for dating and analyzing business cycles across U.S. states, and he has been a CFA charterholder since 2005. He began his career at Goldman Sachs, where he spent seven years, and from 2018 to 2023 was Assistant Professor of Economics and Finance at Canisius University, teaching econometrics and business analytics. He currently serves as Chief Financial Officer of Awecom, a software company.
